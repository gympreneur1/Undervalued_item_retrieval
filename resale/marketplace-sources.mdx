TradeHunter pulls listings from more than 20 auction and marketplace platforms so you never have to check them individually. Each source is crawled on a daily schedule, and new listings are ingested, normalized, and scored before appearing in your deal feed. The platforms span government liquidations, estate sales, foreclosure auctions, business bankruptcies, university surplus programs, and peer-to-peer marketplaces — giving you the widest possible view of below-market inventory across every major secondary-market channel.

## Source Categories

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="Government & Surplus Auctions">
    Government agencies at the federal, state, and local level regularly dispose of surplus assets — vehicles, office furniture, tools, industrial equipment, fitness equipment, electronics, and more — through public auction. TradeHunter monitors the following government and surplus platforms:

    - **GovDeals** — The largest U.S. government surplus marketplace, used by thousands of local municipalities, school districts, and state agencies to auction off decommissioned assets. Listings range from patrol vehicles and lawn equipment to commercial kitchen gear and fitness machines.
    - **GovPlanet** — Specializes in heavy equipment and construction machinery sold by governments and large contractors. Strong source for forklifts, loaders, generators, and utility vehicles.
    - **PublicSurplus** — A government-focused auction platform used by cities, counties, utilities, and public universities across the U.S. Often overlaps with but is distinct from GovDeals.
    - **IronPlanet** — Heavy equipment auctions with a rigorous inspection report system (IronClad Assurance). Useful for contractors and equipment resellers who need verified condition data.
    - **Local City & County Surplus Portals** — Many municipalities run their own surplus disposal portals outside of the major networks. TradeHunter monitors dozens of regional city and county portals, including public works departments and transit authorities that post their own auction schedules.

    Government auctions are particularly valuable because sellers are motivated by disposal, not profit maximization — items frequently sell well below private-market prices, especially when bidder turnout is low.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="University Surplus">
    Colleges and universities continually cycle out lab equipment, office furniture, AV gear, fitness center equipment, and research instruments. Because institutions prioritize campus space and compliance over asset recovery, prices at university surplus sales are often dramatically below replacement cost.

    - **Duke Surplus** — Duke University's surplus property program auctions lab instruments, medical-grade fitness equipment, office furniture, computers, and research tools.
    - **UNC Surplus** — The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill's surplus division disposes of assets ranging from industrial shelving to treadmills and strength equipment.
    - **NC State Surplus** — NC State University's surplus program is notable for its volume of lab and engineering equipment, as well as periodic gym and recreation center asset liquidations.
    - **General University Asset Disposal** — TradeHunter monitors dozens of additional university surplus programs across the U.S., including state university system-wide portals and individual campus programs that post periodic auction events.

    University surplus is an especially strong source for vintage commercial fitness equipment, as many campus recreation centers are upgrading equipment and selling off well-maintained older commercial pieces at a fraction of their resale value.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Estate Sales & Liquidations">
    Estate sales and liquidation companies clear out residential and commercial properties on behalf of families, executors, and businesses. The time pressure inherent in these sales — a property must be cleared by a fixed date — routinely produces strong deals on high-quality items.

    - **EstateSales.net** — The largest estate sale listing aggregator in the U.S., covering thousands of sales per week. TradeHunter monitors listings that include fitness equipment, tools, collectibles, and commercial-grade items from home gym setups and small business closures.
    - **GSOL (Greater Southeast Online Liquidations)** — A regional online liquidation platform serving the Southeast U.S. Strong coverage of commercial equipment, fitness gear, and business inventory from closed businesses and estates.
    - **MaxSold** — An online estate auction platform used by downsizing homeowners, estate executors, and seniors moving to assisted living. Often features well-maintained home gym equipment and vintage commercial pieces.
    - **Caring Transitions** — A nationwide senior relocation and estate liquidation franchise that runs both in-person and online sales. Caring Transitions auctions frequently include commercial-grade fitness equipment from clients who previously operated small gyms or wellness studios.

    When filtering estate sale sources, use keyword filters for brand names, equipment types, or item conditions to surface relevant listings from what are otherwise general-merchandise sales.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Foreclosures & Tax Liens">
    Foreclosure and tax lien auctions allow buyers to acquire real property — and sometimes the business personal property inside it — at court-supervised auction prices. TradeHunter monitors both real property and personal property liquidations tied to foreclosure and tax enforcement actions.

    - **County Sheriff Sales** — When a court orders the sale of a property to satisfy a judgment or mortgage default, the county sheriff typically administers the auction. TradeHunter tracks sheriff sale schedules across dozens of counties, including those that post equipment and personal property alongside real estate.
    - **Auction.com** — One of the largest online platforms for foreclosed real estate auctions in the U.S., including bank-owned (REO) properties and pre-foreclosure auctions. Relevant when a property includes a commercial gym, warehouse, or business space being sold with contents.
    - **RealtyBid** — An online real estate auction platform focused on bank-owned and distressed properties. Often used by community and regional banks to liquidate REO assets quickly.
    - **Tax Deed Auctions** — When property owners fail to pay property taxes, counties eventually sell the property at a tax deed auction. These auctions are county-administered and vary widely by state. TradeHunter monitors active tax deed auction calendars in key markets.

    Foreclosure and tax lien auctions are best used in combination with business bankruptcy sources — when a gym, warehouse, or commercial business closes due to financial distress, both the real property and the equipment inside it may be liquidated through separate but related auction processes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Business Bankruptcies & Lab Equipment">
    When a business files for Chapter 7 bankruptcy liquidation, a court-appointed trustee sells all assets to satisfy creditors. These sales are time-constrained and often priced to clear — making them among the most reliable sources for below-market commercial equipment. TradeHunter also monitors specialized lab and scientific equipment platforms where institutional sellers divest high-value instruments at steep discounts.

    - **BidSpotter** — One of the most widely used platforms for live and online industrial and commercial auctions, including bankruptcy liquidations. TradeHunter monitors BidSpotter for gym equipment, commercial kitchen assets, office furniture, and industrial machinery.
    - **Hilco Industrial** — A major industrial asset liquidator that manages large-scale equipment auctions from manufacturing plants, distribution centers, and commercial businesses. Occasionally lists fitness and recreation equipment from hotel, corporate, and institutional gym closures.
    - **Aaron's Auction** — A regional auction company (common in the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic) that handles business liquidations, estate sales, and commercial equipment auctions.
    - **LabX** — A scientific instrument resale marketplace where universities, research labs, and biotech companies sell decommissioned instruments. Relevant for buyers interested in high-value lab equipment, medical devices, and rehabilitation fitness tools (e.g., isokinetic testing equipment, Cybex clinical units).
    - **BioSurplus** — Specializes in surplus lab and life science equipment from research institutions and biotech companies. Listings include medical-grade fitness and rehabilitation equipment from clinical settings.

    Business bankruptcy auctions frequently produce the best Deal Scores in TradeHunter because sellers are legally obligated to maximize creditor recovery through a transparent competitive process — and bidder awareness is often low for niche items like commercial fitness equipment.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="General Marketplaces">
    Peer-to-peer and general-purpose marketplaces generate enormous daily listing volume. While signal-to-noise is lower than structured auction platforms, the sheer volume means significant deals surface regularly — particularly for locally listed heavy items that attract few online buyers due to shipping constraints.

    - **Facebook Marketplace** — The largest peer-to-peer local marketplace in the U.S. TradeHunter scans publicly accessible Facebook Marketplace listings in your target geographies for equipment matching your keyword and category filters. Local pickup requirements often depress prices on heavy items like commercial gym equipment.
    - **Craigslist** — Still one of the most active local classifieds platforms, particularly for large or heavy items. TradeHunter monitors Craigslist listings across all major U.S. metro areas for items matching your saved searches.
    - **eBay (Buy It Now and Auction)** — TradeHunter monitors both fixed-price and auction-format eBay listings, using eBay's completed sales data as a primary input for market value estimation. Newly listed underpriced Buy It Now items are flagged in real time when they fall within your Deal Score threshold.
    - **OfferUp** — A mobile-first peer-to-peer marketplace with strong adoption in certain U.S. markets. TradeHunter scans OfferUp listings for items matching your saved search criteria.

    For general marketplaces, TradeHunter's value lies in benchmarking: the platform tells you whether a $300 listing for a commercial cable machine is a deal or fair market price, saving you from having to manually cross-reference completed sales.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Specialty Auction Platforms">
    Specialty auction platforms occupy the space between government surplus portals and general marketplaces — they host auctions from independent auctioneers, estate companies, and liquidators, and they attract a mix of professional resellers and retail buyers. TradeHunter monitors the following specialty platforms:

    - **HiBid** — A widely used online auction platform that hosts thousands of live and timed auctions from independent auction companies across the U.S. Strong coverage of commercial equipment, fitness gear, tools, and estate contents.
    - **AuctionZip** — One of the largest directories of auction listings in the U.S., aggregating auctions from independent auctioneers. TradeHunter uses AuctionZip to surface upcoming auctions with lots matching your keyword filters.
    - **Proxibid** — An online bidding platform for professional auctioneers, used heavily for industrial, agricultural, and commercial equipment auctions. TradeHunter monitors Proxibid for relevant lots across all major equipment categories.
    - **PropertyRoom** — A unique auction platform that sells unclaimed property and surplus assets on behalf of law enforcement agencies and government entities. Items include electronics, tools, jewelry, and occasionally fitness equipment seized or surrendered through law enforcement processes.
    - **LiveAuctioneers** — An online bidding platform used by traditional auction houses for fine art, antiques, and collectibles, but also by commercial liquidators. Relevant for vintage and collectible fitness equipment, specialty tools, and high-end resale items.

    Specialty auction platforms often have lower bidder density for niche categories compared to general marketplaces, which creates the conditions for the deepest discounts on items like commercial fitness equipment, lab instruments, and industrial tools.
  </Accordion>
