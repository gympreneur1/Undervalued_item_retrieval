Old-school commercial gym equipment occupies a unique and durable niche in the resale market. While consumer-grade home gym equipment loses value quickly, the heavy-gauge steel, precision cams, and industrial drivetrains found in equipment from brands like Nautilus, MedX, Body Masters, and Cybex make these machines genuinely irreplaceable — the manufacturing standards that produced them no longer exist at any price point. Home gym enthusiasts prize them for their biomechanical precision. Boutique gym owners buy them for their durability and aesthetics. Physical therapists seek out clinical-grade rehabilitation machines that have never been replicated. The result is a secondary market where well-maintained vintage commercial pieces routinely sell for multiples of their acquisition price at auction — if you know where to look and can move quickly when they surface. TradeHunter's Vintage Fitness Equipment filter gives you exactly that edge.

## Condition Filters

When evaluating vintage equipment, condition is everything. TradeHunter surfaces condition information from listing text and auctioneer notes and maps it to four standardized condition tags:

| Condition Tag | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Working** | Seller or auctioneer confirms the item powers on and functions. Most reliable when from a structured auction platform with inspection reports. |
| **Untested** | Condition is unknown — the item has not been tested by the seller. Common in government surplus and estate sales where staff are not qualified to test fitness equipment. Often still a strong deal if the price is low enough. |
| **For Parts** | Item is not functional and is sold for parts or repair only. Useful for buyers who refurbish equipment or need specific components. |
| **Cosmetic Damage Only** | Item is functional but has visible wear, scratches, torn upholstery, or faded paint. Often the best deals — cosmetic restoration is inexpensive and the underlying machine is sound. |

Apply one or more condition filters in the **Condition** dropdown within your Resale search. If you are comfortable refurbishing equipment, including **Cosmetic Damage Only** and **Untested** in your filter set will significantly expand your deal flow.

## Tips for Evaluating Condition from Auction Photos

Auction photos are often the only condition evidence you have before bidding. Here is what to look for when evaluating vintage commercial fitness equipment from photos:

- **Frame and weld points** — Look for rust, cracks, or repairs at weld joints on the frame. Surface rust on cosmetic areas is acceptable; structural rust or cracked welds are a red flag.
- **Weight stacks and selector pins** — Confirm the weight stack is present and complete. Missing plates or a bent selector pin can be costly to replace on older machines with non-standard hardware.
- **Cable and pulleys** — Frayed cables are inexpensive to replace. Check pulley conditions if visible — cracked or seized pulleys add to reconditioning cost.
- **Upholstery** — Torn or deteriorated upholstery is purely cosmetic and straightforward to replace. Do not let bad upholstery deter you from an otherwise sound machine.
- **Cam and pivot hardware** — For Nautilus and similar cam-based machines, look for the characteristic kidney-shaped or spiral cam in photos. Confirm the cam is present and not visibly damaged.
- **Shrouds and covers** — Missing plastic shrouds are cosmetic but can be difficult to source for discontinued models. Note any missing covers in your cost estimate.
- **Serial number plates** — If visible, note the serial number. This can help confirm the exact model and production year before bidding.

When photos are insufficient to assess condition, use the **Ask Auctioneer** feature (available on HiBid, GovDeals, and BidSpotter listings) directly from the TradeHunter listing detail page to request additional photos or inspection information before bidding.

## Brand Reference Guide

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="Nautilus">
    Nautilus is the most recognized name in vintage commercial strength equipment. The original Arthur Jones-era Nautilus machines (1970s–1990s) used a patented spiral cam to maintain constant muscle tension throughout the range of motion — a design that remains biomechanically superior to many modern machines.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Nautilus Nitro series (chest press, lat pulldown, leg press, leg extension, leg curl, row, shoulder press, bicep curl, tricep extension)
    - Nautilus Nitro Plus series (selectorized, heavier builds)
    - Nautilus One series (more recent commercial line)
    - Original Nautilus plate-loaded (pre-1990, highly sought by collectors and home gym builders)
    - Nautilus Multi-Exercise Machine (MEM)

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $75–$400 per piece depending on model, condition, and platform. Full Nautilus Nitro selectorized sets from gym liquidations may run $1,500–$6,000 for 10–15 pieces but represent strong value when individual pieces sell for $400–$800 each at retail resale.

    **Best Sources:** GovDeals, BidSpotter, HiBid, university surplus (campus recreation center liquidations)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="MedX">
    MedX was founded by Arthur Jones (the creator of Nautilus) as a medical and rehabilitation equipment company. MedX machines are built to clinical-grade standards and are used in physical therapy, chiropractic, and sports medicine settings. They are exceptionally rare at auction and command strong prices when they surface.

    **Notable Models:**
    - MedX Lumbar Extension Machine
    - MedX Cervical Extension Machine
    - MedX Knee Extension / Leg Press
    - MedX Rotary Torso
    - MedX Chest Press / Pullover

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $200–$1,200 per piece. MedX lumbar and cervical units in working condition regularly resell to physical therapy practices for $2,000–$5,000+.

    **Best Sources:** BidSpotter, Hilco Industrial, university and hospital surplus, LabX, BioSurplus (clinical rehab equipment)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Body Masters">
    Body Masters produced heavy-duty commercial plate-loaded and selectorized strength equipment throughout the 1980s and 1990s. The brand is known for exceptionally durable welded steel construction and straightforward mechanics that are easy to service.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Body Masters plate-loaded leg press (horizontal and angled)
    - Body Masters seated row / lat pulldown combo
    - Body Masters chest press and pec deck
    - Body Masters cable crossover tower
    - Body Masters Smith machine

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $50–$350 per piece. Well-maintained plate-loaded pieces are popular with home gym builders and small commercial gyms.

    **Best Sources:** HiBid, AuctionZip, estate sales, Facebook Marketplace, regional gym liquidations on BidSpotter
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Nebula">
    Nebula manufactures heavy-gauge plate-loaded and functional training equipment designed for serious strength training environments. Nebula pieces are prized for their industrial-grade construction and are frequently found in powerlifting and strength-focused commercial gyms.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Nebula 45-degree leg press
    - Nebula hack squat machine
    - Nebula glute ham raise
    - Nebula preacher curl bench
    - Nebula functional trainer / cable machine

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $100–$500 per piece at auction. Nebula leg press and hack squat machines are consistently in demand from home gym builders.

    **Best Sources:** HiBid, BidSpotter, Facebook Marketplace, gym bankruptcy liquidations
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Atlantis Strength">
    Atlantis Strength is a Canadian commercial gym equipment manufacturer known for biomechanically refined selectorized and plate-loaded machines used in premium commercial facilities. Atlantis equipment is less common at auction than U.S.-produced brands but commands strong interest when it surfaces.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Atlantis A-Line selectorized series
    - Atlantis P-Line plate-loaded series
    - Atlantis functional trainers
    - Atlantis Smith machine

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $150–$600 per piece. Full Atlantis commercial selectorized sets are rare but can represent exceptional value.

    **Best Sources:** BidSpotter, Hilco Industrial, hotel and corporate gym liquidations
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cybex">
    Cybex has produced commercial gym equipment since the 1970s, with product lines spanning rehabilitation-grade isokinetic dynamometers, clinical strength equipment, and mainstream commercial cardio and strength lines. Vintage Cybex clinical equipment is particularly sought after by physical therapists and researchers.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Cybex Eagle selectorized series (chest, back, shoulder, leg lines)
    - Cybex VR2 series (late 1990s–early 2000s selectorized)
    - Cybex 770T and 625T treadmills (older commercial cardio)
    - Cybex NORM isokinetic dynamometer (clinical — highly specialized)
    - Cybex Bravo functional trainer

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $75–$400 per piece for strength equipment. Cybex NORM and isokinetic clinical units can fetch $1,000–$5,000+ when operational.

    **Best Sources:** University surplus, hospital liquidations, BidSpotter, LabX (for clinical units), GovDeals
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Hammer Strength">
    Hammer Strength (acquired by LifeFitness in 1997) produces plate-loaded and MTS (Modular Training System) commercial equipment that has become a standard in professional sports facilities, collegiate weight rooms, and premium gyms. Older Hammer Strength plate-loaded pieces are among the most consistently resaleable vintage equipment on the market.

    **Notable Models:**
    - Hammer Strength ISO-Lateral Leg Press
    - Hammer Strength ISO-Lateral Incline Press
    - Hammer Strength ISO-Lateral Row
    - Hammer Strength Ground Base Jammer
    - Hammer Strength MTS Functional Row
    - Hammer Strength 4-Way Neck Machine

    **Typical Auction Price Range:** $150–$700 per piece at auction. ISO-Lateral Leg Press units in good condition resell for $800–$1,500. Hammer Strength pieces from university athletic facility liquidations represent some of the highest-ROI opportunities TradeHunter regularly surfaces.

    **Best Sources:** University surplus (athletic facilities), BidSpotter, HiBid, GovDeals, Hilco Industrial
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Icarian, David, and LifeFitness Vintage Commercial">
    These three brand families cover specialized niches within the vintage commercial equipment market:

    **Icarian** — Produced heavy-duty plate-loaded and selectorized commercial equipment in the 1980s and 1990s (later acquired). Known for robust cable systems and compound multi-function units. Typical auction price: $50–$250 per piece.

    **David (Rehabilitation)** — A Finnish rehabilitation equipment company producing precision lumbar, cervical, and functional fitness systems for clinical and therapeutic use. David equipment is rare at auction and commands high prices from physical therapy practices. Typical auction price: $300–$1,500+ per unit.

    **LifeFitness Vintage Commercial** — Older commercial LifeFitness treadmills, steppers, ellipticals, and strength equipment from hotel, corporate, and institutional gym disposals. Earlier commercial generations (pre-2005) are durable and in demand for budget commercial facilities. Typical auction price: $75–$400 per piece.

    **Best Sources for all three:** Hotel FF&E liquidations, BidSpotter, Hilco Industrial, HiBid, university surplus

<Tip>
  Some of the highest-ROI opportunities in vintage gym equipment come not from single-piece listings but from bundled gym lots — a business bankruptcy or university athletic facility liquidation that puts 15–30 pieces of commercial equipment into a single auction lot. These lots often attract fewer bids than individual pieces because buyers worry about logistics, storage, and moving unwanted items. If you have the truck, the space, and the ability to break a lot into individual resale pieces, bundled lots can multiply your effective ROI dramatically. In TradeHunter, use the **Lot Type: Bundle / Multi-Item** filter in the Resale search to surface these opportunities, and set your geographic filter tightly since moving large multi-piece lots over long distances quickly erodes margin.
</Tip>
