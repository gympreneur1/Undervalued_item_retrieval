For certain commercial uses — gas stations, quick-service restaurants, auto services, billboards, distribution hubs, and destination retail — highway visibility is not a nice-to-have; it is the primary driver of a site's income potential. A parcel that thousands of motorists see every day commands dramatically higher rents, lower vacancy risk, and a more liquid resale market than an equivalent site tucked behind a tree line. TradeHunter's Highway Visibility filter brings precision to this search: instead of scrolling through dozens of listings and manually checking their proximity to I-40 or I-85, you define the corridor, the visibility type, and the minimum frontage, and TradeHunter returns only the parcels that genuinely deliver the exposure you need — across every connected data source simultaneously.

## What Highway Visibility Means in TradeHunter

TradeHunter classifies highway visibility into three tiers, each reflecting a different level of motorist exposure:

**Direct Frontage**
The parcel shares a legal boundary with the highway right-of-way and has legal access (curb cut, shared driveway, or ramp access) to the corridor. This is the most valuable tier for uses that depend on impulse or convenience traffic.

**Visibility Corridor (Within ¼ Mile)**
The parcel is located within 1,320 feet of the highway centerline and has unobstructed line-of-sight to the travel lanes (verified via satellite imagery and terrain analysis). The structure or signage on the parcel would be visible to highway motorists even without direct frontage. Suitable for uses where brand awareness and wayfinding signage matter — hotels, regional retail, medical facilities, storage facilities.

**Interchange Proximity (Within ½ Mile of On/Off Ramp)**
The parcel is within half a mile of a highway interchange, capturing high-capture-rate traffic entering or exiting the highway. Particularly valuable for food and beverage, fuel, lodging, and convenience-oriented uses whose customers are already in a decision-making mindset as they approach the exit.

## Enabling the Highway Visibility Filter

You can apply the Highway Visibility filter in any property search, or use it as a standalone starting point for a new search. Here is how to set it up:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a New or Existing Property Search">
    Navigate to **Commercial Real Estate → New Property Search**, or open a saved search you want to refine. The Highway Visibility controls are located in the **Location & Access** section of the filter panel on the left side of the search page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable Highway Visibility">
    Toggle the **Highway Visibility** switch to **On**. Three sub-filters appear immediately below: **Highway Corridor**, **Visibility Type**, and **Minimum Frontage (ft)**. The search will not execute the highway visibility analysis until you configure at least one of these three sub-filters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Your Target Highway Corridors">
    Click the **Highway Corridor** dropdown and select one or more corridors from the list. You can select all corridors in the 336 area by clicking **Select All 336-Area Highways**, or choose individual corridors — for example, selecting only **I-40** and **NC-68** to focus on the airport corridor and the primary east-west spine.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Visibility Type">
    In the **Visibility Type** dropdown, select the tier(s) you will accept:
    - **Direct Frontage Only** — Strictest filter; returns only parcels with boundary contact with the highway ROW.
    - **Frontage + Visibility Corridor** — Returns direct frontage and parcels within ¼ mile with confirmed line-of-sight.
    - **All Types** — Includes interchange proximity parcels within ½ mile. Broadest filter; best for first-pass market research.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Minimum Frontage (Optional)">
    If your intended use requires a specific minimum road frontage — for example, a gas station or drive-through restaurant typically needs at least 150 feet of frontage to accommodate curb cuts and stacking lanes — enter that value in the **Minimum Frontage (ft)** field. TradeHunter will exclude parcels whose highway-facing frontage is below your threshold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply Remaining Search Filters">
    Configure your other search filters as normal: property type (typically **Land** or **Retail** for highway-visible searches), price range, lot size, and any additional filters. For highway-visible land acquisition, consider enabling the **Zoning: Commercial / Highway Commercial** filter to exclude residentially zoned parcels that may not be developable for your intended use without a rezoning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run the Search and Review the Map">
    Click **Search Properties**. In addition to the standard results table, switch to **Map View** to see each parcel plotted against the highway corridor overlay. Parcels are color-coded by visibility tier: orange for Direct Frontage, blue for Visibility Corridor, and purple for Interchange Proximity. The highway right-of-way boundaries and interchange ramp footprints are shown as gray overlays so you can immediately see each parcel's spatial relationship to the corridor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Your Search for New Listing Alerts">
    Once your filters are dialed in, click **Save Search** and give it a name (e.g., "I-40 Direct Frontage Land – 336"). Set your alert frequency to **Real-Time** or **Daily Digest**. TradeHunter will notify you the moment a new parcel matching your highway visibility criteria comes to market — before it has time to accumulate broad exposure on LoopNet or CoStar.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Using the Map View to Evaluate Highway Parcels

The map view provides context that the results table cannot. When reviewing highway-visible parcels, use these map features:

- **Traffic Heatmap Overlay** — Toggle on the **Traffic Volume** layer to display a color-coded heatmap of NCDOT traffic count data across the corridor. Deep red segments indicate the highest-volume stretches, which typically command premium land pricing and the strongest tenant demand.
- **Interchange Detail** — Zoom into any interchange to see the on-ramp and off-ramp geometry, the acreage of each quadrant, and which quadrants currently have active listings or recent sales. Dominant quadrants (those with the highest visibility to approaching and exiting traffic) are labeled.
- **Zoning Overlay** — Toggle on **Zoning** to see municipal and county zoning designations for each parcel. Highway Business (HB), General Commercial (GC), and Industrial (I-1/I-2) zones are color-coded distinctly from residential zones.
- **Sold Comps Layer** — Toggle on **Recent Sales** to see land transactions from the past 36 months along the corridor, displayed as price-per-acre markers. This gives you an immediate visual benchmark for what highway-visible land has been trading for in each interchange area.
- **Satellite + Street View** — Click any parcel pin and select **Street View** to see the actual sightline from the highway travel lane toward the parcel. This is the fastest way to gut-check whether a "visibility corridor" parcel actually has meaningful exposure or whether vegetation, grade changes, or competing signage limit motorist awareness.

## Setting Up a Saved Search for New Highway-Visible Listings

Highway-visible commercial land in the 336 metro trades quickly, particularly along the I-40 and NC-68 corridors near Piedmont Triad International Airport. A saved search with real-time alerts ensures you see new listings before they receive broad market exposure:

1. Complete your search setup using the steps above.
2. Click **Save Search** → name it clearly (e.g., "I-40 / I-85 Frontage Land – Any Size").
3. Set **Alert Frequency** to **Real-Time**.
4. Enable **Price Drop Alerts** to be notified when an existing listing reduces its asking price.
5. Under **Advanced Alert Settings**, enable **New Listing Source Notifications** — this tells you which platform the listing appeared on first, which can help you identify the originating broker and reach them before the listing is widely distributed.

<Tip>
  Combine the Highway Visibility filter with the **Tax Incentive: Opportunity Zone** filter to find highway-visible commercial land that also sits within a designated OZ census tract. Several interchange quadrants along I-40 in Guilford County and US-421 in Forsyth County fall within OZ boundaries — meaning you could pair the traffic exposure of a highway-fronting site with the capital gains tax benefits of an Opportunity Zone investment. Run this combined filter and sort the results by **Discount % (Highest First)** to identify the most compelling three-factor deals: highway visibility + OZ status + below-market pricing.
</Tip>
